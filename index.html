The moon illusion

A low moon looks huge because your brain compares it with trees and buildings, but it is really the same size all night. A phone's wide lens records the true size, so it comes out as a dot. This app finds the moon and enlarges it to match what your eyes felt.

Big-moon alerts

Get a phone alert before each low-moon window from the Moon Mind's Eye iPhone app. Only your phone gets them.

👇 Alerts are turned on inside the app, not on this website.

In the app: open Moon Mind's Eye → Moon tab → Big-moon alerts → turn on Alert me, then tap Allow for notifications.

No app yet? It's in testing through TestFlight. Ask the developer for an invite.

Live camera

  • Tap Start live camera and choose Allow when asked.
  • Point at the moon. A gold ring or Moon locked ✓ means it found it. If it picks a streetlight, tap the moon.
  • Use 2x, 3x or 5x and keep trees or rooftops in the frame. Step back from them: the farther away they are, the bigger the moon looks next to them.
  • Hold still. The steps under the picture check themselves off, and the camera counts 3-2-1 and takes the photo for you.

Or use the iPhone Camera

  • Get trees, rooftops or a skyline in the frame. The foreground makes the moon feel big.
  • Use 2x, 3x or 5x zoom if your iPhone has it.
  • Touch and hold the moon to lock focus, then drag the brightness slider down until the moon shows detail.
  • Take 3–5 shots in a row and pick them all. The app merges them to remove grain.
